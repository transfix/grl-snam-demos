GRL-SNAM · deliverable · August 2026

Fog of war, at the scale of a real city

A squad of vehicles crossing a kilometre of real Austin, each planning on its own private map, discovering the city — and each other — through a sensor. Every frame replays a recorded run. Nothing on this page is a re-simulation, and nothing is an artist's impression.

01What this is

The earlier deliverable showed one vehicle on a map that was allowed to be wrong. This one takes the same machinery to a real city and to a squad, and adds the thing that was hardest to show honestly: what the vehicles could actually see.

The geometry is OpenStreetMap buildings on SRTM terrain, rasterised at 3.13 m per cell. Each agent runs its own belief grid, its own route, its own distance field. Nothing is shared between them except the ground truth they are both standing in — and each other: a vehicle occludes and has to be discovered, exactly like a wall.

02The three runs

Each is one continuous recorded run, rendered offscreen through the same cvcGL scene renderer the interactive tool uses.

03Seeing what the vehicles saw

Three separate things are drawn, and they answer different questions.

The field of view

The ground carries the recorded sensor coverage as a texture: never seen, seen and remembered, visible right now. It is the true ray-cast set, with the ragged occlusion shadows behind buildings — not a disc, which would be a lie about occlusion.

The walls

A face of a building is lit only if a ray actually reached that face. Drive past a tower and its lower storeys light up; its roof and its far side do not.

The paths

Each vehicle's driven path in its own colour. The planned route appears only in the overhead inset — see the note below, which is a point about honesty rather than about layout.

The overhead inset

A fixed orthographic map of the whole area. Orthographic on purpose: under perspective a marker held above the skyline projects away from the vehicle it belongs to, by up to 169 m at the map edge.

Why the planned route is only on the map. The planner string-pulls its route through believed free space before committing to it. Early in a run the belief is empty, so the committed route is a single straight segment more than a kilometre long — which is exactly right, because the planner has not looked yet and genuinely believes that line is clear. On a 2-D map that reads as intent. Drawn over real buildings in the 3-D shot it reads as a rendering bug. It is shown where it tells the truth and omitted where it would mislead.

04Which navigator is driving, and whether it matters

There is one local controller — a distance-field follower with a wall-following escape — and one optional addition: an A* route spine planned over the agent's own belief, feeding it a lookahead sub-goal. Every clip here uses the spine, and says so in its heads-up display. They are not interchangeable.

Percentages are driven distance against the straight line. Same map, same seed, same controller — the route spine is the only difference.

The direction flips with complexity. On a map with a single obstacle the route spine is a tax: the reactive controller goes nearly straight and beats it by 2–3%. Once there is topology or motion it is worth 2–3.4x. On the real city the reactive controller does not solve the problem at all — 941 m driven against a 1635 m straight line, sitting in a local minimum, never arriving. Zero collisions in every case, both ways: the difference is whether it arrives, not whether it is safe.

The convoy's contact count is real, and it is new. Followers closing on a vehicle they are chasing do touch it. That number could not have appeared before this release: peers were being written into each other's world and then erased, every tick, by the line that resets ground truth — so the squad was mutually invisible and a vehicle-to-vehicle collision could not be counted even in principle. It is counted now.

06What was built for this

  • A squad with private beliefs. One scenario per agent over one shared truth. Coverage is shared; knowledge is not.
  • Agents that are real to each other. Peers are stamped into each other's ground truth as a mask that survives the per-tick reset, so they occlude, are discovered by a sensor ray, and are scored in collisions.
  • Targets that are other vehicles. A goal can now be another agent, read live. A scripted path cannot serve as a convoy leader or as prey, because where the vehicle in front will be is not knowable in advance — it is reacting to a world it is still discovering. Scripted targets also walked through buildings; vehicles route around them.
  • A camera that solves for the shot. It searches bearings each frame and scores them on whether the vehicles are visible, not on whether the line to their centre is. Blocked rays fell from 52.0% to 9.3%, and the mean corrective climb from 69.2 m to 3.5 m — which is why it stays in the city instead of hanging above it.
  • Wall visibility by ray-casting the real mesh. A post-pass over the finished trace, so the simulation is untouched.
  • Vehicles that park. Sitting on a goal, the direction to it is numerical noise, so a vehicle used to pirouette on the spot. It now brakes and holds heading.

07Honest limits

  • Wall visibility is a rendering input, not something the agents knew. The planner's sensor is a 2-D ray cast over an occupancy raster and carries no height. The mesh cast replays recorded positions under a stated sensor model to answer "which surface was in view". No planning decision used it.
  • One agent in the dispersal scene stalls 263.7 m short. Not a collision and not a planner failure — the local controller gives up when the route keeps changing under it. It is the one agent in the set that does not finish.
  • Real-time is not there yet. The loop runs at 0.73 Hz for 8 agents on a 384² grid. The gap to 30 Hz is 41x and is understood in detail: 93.6% of a tick is in three functions, neither hot one is slow for algorithmic reasons, and a staged plan is written up in the public repository's docs/PERFORMANCE.md.

08Run it yourself

From the public GRL-SNAM repository. pip install on its own is not enough: the renderer binding pycvc_gl is a cvcpkg package and is not on PyPI, so the dependency closure comes from the per-interpreter recipe in the repo. This is what CI does.

cvcpkg install cvc/grl-snam-cp311 --prefix ./deps
source ./deps/bin/activate

grl-snam fog all -o fog/            # the three synthetic stories

# `finale` is newer than the published grl-snam-cp311 0.1.0+cvc.1. Until that
# is republished, run it from a checkout on top of the same activated prefix:
pip install -e . --no-deps          # the closure is already in the prefix
grl-snam finale BUNDLE -o finale/   # the city-scale runs

Use the -cp312 / -cp313 recipe and the matching python3.1x for those interpreters.

The simulation runs exactly once, in record. Every video, every live window and every number quoted here replays that measured trace and recomputes nothing — which is why the film cannot disagree with the run it claims to show.
